About the study

Paper title

Maternity Care Within Reach: Mapping Spatial Accessibility and Regional Inequalities Across the Former Yugoslav Republics

Authors and affiliations

Filip Arnaut, Sebastian Rauch, Ljiljana Pantović, Sreten Jevremović

Institute of Physics Belgrade, National Institute of the Republic of Serbia, Belgrade, Serbia (F.A., S.J.)

Institute for Empirical Sociology, FAU Erlangen-Nuremberg (S.R.)

Institute for Philosophy and Social Theory, University of Belgrade, Belgrade, Serbia (Lj.P.)

Abstract

Spatial proximity to maternity care facilities is a critical dimension of healthcare accessibility, yet substantial inequalities may remain concealed behind national coverage estimates. This study assessed spatial accessibility to 158 maternity care facilities across six former Yugoslav republics: Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, and North Macedonia. Driving-time isochrones were generated using OpenRouteService at 30- and 60-minute thresholds and integrated with WorldPop demographic rasters to estimate accessibility for women of reproductive age (15- 49 years). Regional inequalities, urban- rural disparities, and sensitivity to ±5-minute threshold adjustments were also examined. The results revealed considerable spatial variation, with national 30-minute accessibility ranging from 70.42% in Croatia to 88.74% in North Macedonia. Across the study area, approximately 919,000 women lived beyond 30 minutes of a maternity care facility, while 135,000 remained beyond 60 minutes. Urban centers exhibited nearly universal 30-minute accessibility, whereas rural clusters showed substantially lower coverage, ranging from 42.82% in Bosnia and Herzegovina to 66.39% in North Macedonia. These findings demonstrate that high national accessibility coverage can conceal substantial regional and urban- rural inequalities. By integrating high-resolution population data, network-based accessibility modelling, and threshold sensitivity analysis, this study provides a reproducible framework for identifying geographically underserved populations and supporting evidence-based maternity healthcare planning across Europe.
